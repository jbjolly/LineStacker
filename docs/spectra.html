Stacking spectra¶

LineStacker.OneD_Stacker stacks spectra that were already extracted. Each spectrum is a LineStacker.OneD_Stacker.Spectrum, which knows where its line is; LineStacker.OneD_Stacker.Stack() aligns the spectra on their lines and averages them channel by channel.

import numpy as np
import LineStacker.OneD_Stacker as OneD

spectra = []
for i in range(100):
    data = np.loadtxt('data/spectra_%d' % i)        # columns: velocity, flux
    centre = np.loadtxt('data/central_velocity_%d' % i)
    spectra.append(OneD.Spectrum(spectrum=data, centralVelocity=centre,
                                 name=str(i)))

stack, nspectra = OneD.Stack(spectra)

Stack returns the stacked spectrum and the number of spectra in each of its channels. Channels without data (no spectrum covers them, or only blank fluxes or spectra of weight 0) are NaN.

Spectra¶

A spectrum is given either as a two-column array, spectral values then flux (shape [N, 2] or [2, N]): Spectrum(spectrum=data); or as fluxes alone, with or without their spectral values: Spectrum(amp=flux, velocities=v). velOrFreq='vel' (default) or 'freq' says whether the spectral values are velocities or frequencies. Velocities are in km/s. The spectral values must increase or decrease evenly (within 5% of a channel, so values rounded in a text file are fine): channels are located with one channel width, the mean spacing. Resample spectra on other grids (log-spaced, with gaps) to an even grid first.

The line centre comes from, in this order:

  • z= and fEmLine=, the redshift and the rest frequency of the line. For frequency spectra the line is at fEmLine/(1+z). For velocity spectra the velocities are relative to the rest frequency (as a CASA spectrum made with restfreq=fEmLine), and the line is at v = c z/(1+z) in the radio convention (velConvention='radio', default, as CASA uses), c z with 'optical', or the relativistic velocity with 'relativistic';

  • centralVelocity= or centralFrequency=, the observed line centre;

  • centerIndex=, its channel;

  • fit=True: a Gaussian fit of the spectrum, which overrides the above.

weights is the weight of the spectrum in mean stacks: a number (default 1), an array of one weight per channel, 'sigma2' (1/σ² of the spectrum, or of the channels outside the line if fit=True), or '1/A' (one over the line amplitude: the maximum, or the fitted amplitude). Channels whose flux is not finite (NaN, inf) are left out. A spectrum needs at least two channels, and as many spectral values as fluxes. name labels the spectrum, for subsampling for instance.

Stack¶

stack, nspectra = OneD.Stack(spectra, method='median', center='fit',
                             chansStack=41)

  • method: 'mean' (weighted) or 'median' (unweighted).

  • center: 'lineCenterIndex' (default) uses the centre each spectrum was given; 'fit' fits a Gaussian to every spectrum; 'zero_vel' uses the channel at velocity 0; 'center' the middle channel; an integer, that channel for all spectra.

  • chansStack: 'full' (default) covers every channel of every spectrum, aligned on the lines; an integer stacks that many channels around the line. The line centre of every spectrum must be one of its channels (an error says which spectrum it is not).

  • regridFromZ=True resamples the spectra so that all have the same rest-frame channel width (see LineStacker.analysisTools.regridFromZ1D()); every spectrum then needs its z. Channels and line centres are scaled by (1+z)/(1+z_ref); with 'scaleToMin' the last channels of the regridded spectra can fall beyond their data and are then NaN.
